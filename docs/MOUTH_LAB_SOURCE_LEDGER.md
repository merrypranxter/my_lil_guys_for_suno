# Mouth Lab Source Ledger

## Purpose

This ledger records research used specifically by Mouth Lab when the module promotes a language profile into a transferable donor trait.

Mouth Lab is a creative vocal-design system. It may transfer **mechanisms** such as suffix chaining, root-pattern interlocking, click categories, vowel harmony, tone, timing, or consonant clustering into an invented vocal organism.

It must not claim that a hybrid output is authentic speech in the donor language.

The existing phonology foundation remains:

- `docs/LANGUAGE_PHONOLOGY_SOURCE_LEDGER.md`
- `src/data/languageProfiles.ts`

This ledger adds research for traits that go beyond that phonology-only layer.

# Morphology research — first pass

## Cherokee

Primary community source:

Cherokee Nation / Cherokee Language Department  
**Learning to Use the Cherokee Verb**  
Durbin Feeling, Joshua Webster, Craig Kopris, Charles van Tuyl  
https://language.cherokee.org/media/vnihnhms/learning-to-use-the-cherokee-verb.pdf

Relevant observations from the book:

- Cherokee verb inflections are formed very differently from English.
- Modifying material that English often expresses with separate words can be joined to the Cherokee verb root.
- Cherokee verbs are described as agglutinative.
- A complete verb may include a pronominal prefix, verb root, stem/mode suffix, and tense suffix.
- Subject/object prefixes can encode both subject and object.
- Roots do not simply stand alone; they combine with required prefixes/suffixes.

Mouth Lab abstraction:

**BOUND VERB PACKING**

The transferable mechanism is not “write fake Cherokee.”

It is:

> allow information that English normally distributes across several words to exert pressure toward one larger bound vocal unit with ordered functional layers around a stable root.

Safe creative use:

- compress an English semantic clause into fewer, larger vocal units;
- add successive functional layers around a preserved lexical anchor;
- make each added layer change timing/density;
- use subject/object/tense-role packing as a structural inspiration without claiming the generated surface form is Cherokee.

Do not claim:

- that arbitrary English morphemes become valid Cherokee by concatenation;
- that all Cherokee morphology is captured by one template;
- that a generated hybrid is grammatically Cherokee.

## Inuktitut

Academic source:

University of Washington Department of Linguistics  
**Featured Language: Inuktitut**  
https://linguistics.washington.edu/news/2020/10/02/featured-language-inuktitut

Relevant observations:

- Inuktitut is described as polysynthetic.
- Subjects, objects, and other information may be expressed through long chains of suffixes attached to a root.
- Some Inuktitut words correspond to what English would express as whole sentences.
- Root/suffix contact can trigger substantial morphophonemic changes.

Mouth Lab abstraction:

**POLYSYNTHETIC SUFFIX CHAIN**

Transferable mechanism:

> start from a lexical root and accumulate functional chunks in a controlled order so one vocal word carries increasing clause-level information.

Safe creative use:

- make repeated phrases grow by suffix-like functional units;
- let larger word forms consume longer rhythmic spans;
- let boundary/contact rules mutate sounds as chains grow.

Do not claim:

- that invented English-derived chains are valid Inuktitut;
- that one generic suffix order represents every Inuktitut dialect;
- that every long word is freely compositional without morphophonemic constraints.

## Turkish

Academic source:

F. Nihan Ketrez  
**A Student Grammar of Turkish — Introduction**  
Cambridge University Press  
https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/student-grammar-of-turkish/introduction/5C534399EFE43902C585A533DE92F6E6

Relevant observations:

- Turkish is agglutinating.
- Multiple suffixes can attach to a stem.
- Long words can be built from ordered suffixes.
- Everyday speech usually uses fewer suffixes than deliberately extreme textbook examples.

Mouth Lab abstraction:

**AGGLUTINATIVE CHAIN**

Transferable mechanism:

> preserve a recognizable stem while successive suffix-like units add one grammatical/function layer at a time.

Safe creative use:

- create visibly incremental word growth;
- make each added unit trigger a musical or rhythmic consequence;
- combine this with vowel harmony so the chain changes internally as it grows.

Do not claim:

- that an arbitrary invented suffix chain is Turkish;
- that maximal chains are representative of ordinary conversational frequency.

## Arabic — Modern Standard profile

Academic source:

Karin C. Ryding  
**Arabic: A Linguistic Introduction — Derivational morphology: the root/pattern system**  
Cambridge University Press  
https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/arabic/derivational-morphology-the-rootpattern-system/DBE7A3A92000E1F1AA40C23DD68B17EE

Supporting reference:

Karin C. Ryding  
**A Reference Grammar of Modern Standard Arabic**  
https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/reference-grammar-of-modern-standard-arabic/207FA6CB610D541184C4B8CB10A4084A/listing

Relevant observations:

- Systematic derivation from lexical roots is central to Arabic word creation.
- Many stems can be analyzed through discontinuous/interlocked roots and patterns.
- Patterns interlock with root phonemes rather than behaving only as simple left-to-right suffix chains.
- Arabic also contains non-root/pattern morphology; the root-pattern system is important but not the whole lexicon.

Mouth Lab abstraction:

**ROOT / PATTERN INTERLOCK**

Transferable mechanism:

> preserve a consonantal or segmental identity skeleton while changing the interleaved vowel/template pattern so related forms remain recognizably descended from the same root.

Safe creative use:

- keep a recurring consonant skeleton while vowel shapes change;
- let one skeleton acquire several rhythmic/melodic templates;
- use the pattern as a transformation law rather than “Arabic-looking” spelling.

Do not claim:

- that every Arabic word is generated exclusively by a three-consonant root;
- that invented English root-pattern forms are authentic Arabic;
- that one MSA abstraction represents every Arabic variety.

# Fidelity policy for morphology transfer

1. **Import mechanisms, not costume.**
2. A real-language donor never authorizes fake “native” text.
3. When Mouth Lab uses a donor rule on English material, label it internally as **derived / inspired structural pressure**, not authentic donor-language grammar.
4. Preserve distinctions between:
   - suffix chaining;
   - polysynthetic clause packing;
   - root-pattern interlocking;
   - phonological harmony;
   - simple concatenation.
5. If a mechanism is not supported by the source ledger, do not add it merely because the language is popularly described as “complex.”
6. Community-authored/community-hosted documentation is preferred when available for Indigenous-language morphology.
7. The user may select several donors simultaneously, but each donor must earn a distinct jurisdiction.
